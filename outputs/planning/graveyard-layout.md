# Graveyard opening layout proposal

The selected direction combines A’s orange terrain and distant light with B’s heavy sky, plus a graveyard of repeated machine wrecks. The following spatial arrangement is a proposal, not an accepted map or authored scene.

## Three connected spaces

**Wake pocket.** Place the stage-two rover in a shallow hollow, surrounded by a few nearby wrecks at different angles. Leave clear ground immediately in front and enough room to turn. One eye remains usable, and the damaged rover must retain enough movement to reach its repair. Avoid starting beneath a dense pile that requires a separate debris-physics system.

**Recovery lane.** A gentle bend between low wreck piles leads to the optical-module interaction. Broken chassis and exposed rock define the route through shape and height. Use a distinctive mechanical outline or a pale exposed surface to identify the recovery point. The module’s source is unresolved: it could be the rover’s detached equipment or a compatible part from another machine. Reserve the space without silently committing to either story.

**Basin shoulder.** Beyond the repair area, a low rise reveals orange terrain and repeated wreck silhouettes fading toward the distant light beneath the heavier sky. This frames a larger world while keeping only the near route traversable. The shoulder can become the first-scene exit; it need not establish the complete game’s length or final destination.

## Route and camera checks

Keep the route at least two rover widths across initially, with a wider turning area at the repair point. Validate clearances using the real model, damaged movement and camera rather than treating this ratio as proof. Keep wrecks below the sightline near the interaction; taller silhouettes belong at the sides and farther away.

Gameplay camera remains undecided. Preserve rear and side clearance for a possible third-person orbit, and a readable approach for a possible rover-camera view. If third person is selected, impaired vision should affect sensing or the rover’s behavior rather than arbitrarily blurring an external viewpoint.

## One-day asset approach

Reuse one wreck chassis with several poses, plus separate wheels, a bent mast, two panel forms and three rock shapes. Near wrecks need readable geometry; distant wrecks can be simple silhouettes. Vary rotation, partial burial and missing parts while retaining recognizable kinship with the protagonist. Use static wrecks and simple collision boundaries; keep loose physics debris outside this first pass.

Let the pale rover remain distinguishable from orange ground. Preserve cold neutral fill under the heavy sky so its eyes and the repair action remain visible. Sparse lateral dust can support the exposed setting without hiding the optical-recovery route.
